# Phils - Product Requirements Document

**Product:** Phils
**Platform:** Android
**Deployment:** Fully self-hosted on user's own server
**Status:** MVP Product Requirements
**Version:** 1.0

---

## 1. Product Overview

Phils is an Android application designed to transform the familiar habit of vertical scrolling into a method of discovering interesting ideas.

Instead of scrolling through short-form entertainment content, users vertically swipe through visually designed cards containing:

* Philosophies
* Philosophers
* Psychological concepts
* Paradoxes
* Thought experiments
* Scientific ideas
* Historical ideas
* Other intellectually interesting concepts

The experience should feel as effortless as scrolling through a short-form content feed, while the content encourages curiosity and deeper exploration.

The core product principle is:

> **Don't make people stop scrolling. Make the scrolling worth doing.**

Phils should not feel like a traditional educational application, encyclopedia, or course. It should feel like an **intellectual discovery feed**.

---

# 2. Problem Statement

Modern short-form content platforms make discovering information extremely easy, but much of the interaction is optimized for entertainment and repeated consumption.

Users can spend significant amounts of time scrolling without intentionally learning or retaining anything meaningful.

Traditional educational applications solve a different problem: they require users to deliberately choose a subject, course, lesson, or article.

Phils sits between these two models.

It uses the familiar:

> **Open → Swipe → Discover → Swipe → Discover**

interaction model while replacing entertainment-first content with intellectually stimulating ideas.

---

# 3. Product Vision

Phils should become a place where a user can open the application with no particular topic in mind and consistently encounter something they did not know before.

The user should be able to say:

> "I opened this for five minutes and discovered five things I actually wanted to know more about."

The product should optimize for:

1. Curiosity
2. Discovery
3. Exploration
4. Serendipity
5. Retention of interesting ideas

It should **not** optimize primarily for:

* Social validation
* Followers
* Likes
* Engagement bait
* Infinite entertainment
* Aggressive notifications

---

# 4. Target User

## Primary User

People who enjoy discovering interesting ideas but do not necessarily consider themselves students, academics, or philosophy enthusiasts.

The target user may:

* Scroll Instagram/Reels/Shorts frequently
* Enjoy learning random interesting facts
* Be curious about philosophy, psychology, science, history, and human behavior
* Prefer short explanations before committing to deeper reading
* Enjoy discovering unexpected connections between ideas

The user does not need prior knowledge.

A person who knows nothing about Stoicism should be able to understand the first Phils card immediately.

---

# 5. Core Product Principle

## Discovery Before Depth

The feed should provide enough information to create curiosity without explaining everything.

Example:

### Feed Card

> **WHAT IF YOU COULD CONTROL YOUR REACTION, BUT NOT THE EVENT?**
>
> **STOICISM**
>
> *A philosophy built around mastering your response to life rather than controlling life itself.*
>
> **Explore philosophy →**

The card should answer:

> "What is this?"

but intentionally leave the user asking:

> "Tell me more."

---

# 6. MVP Goals

The MVP must prove that the core interaction works.

### Goal 1 - Replace passive scrolling with idea discovery

Users should be able to continuously swipe through interesting content.

### Goal 2 - Create curiosity

Cards should make users want to investigate certain ideas.

### Goal 3 - Enable deeper exploration

A user interested in a discovery should be able to tap it and explore a detailed page.

### Goal 4 - Connect ideas

Users should be able to move from one concept to related or contrasting concepts.

### Goal 5 - Build a personal collection

Users should be able to save interesting discoveries.

### Goal 6 - Demonstrate recommendation logic

The MVP should provide a feed that intelligently selects content rather than simply displaying database records sequentially.

---

# 9. Feed Experience

The feed is the central feature of Phils.

## Feed Layout

The feed should occupy essentially the entire Android screen.

Each discovery appears as a visually designed card.

The user primarily interacts through vertical swiping.

### Interaction

* Swipe up → next discovery
* Swipe down → previous discovery
* Tap → open discovery details
* Save button → save discovery
* Information/action menu → explain recommendation

The experience should feel fluid and natural.

---

# 10. Feed Card Design

The feed card should prioritize typography, hierarchy, and visual identity.

A typical card contains:

1. Curiosity-driven hook
2. Discovery title
3. One-line explanation
4. Optional attribution/identity information
5. Explore affordance
6. Save action

Example:

> **WHAT IF YOU COULD CONTROL YOUR REACTION, BUT NOT THE EVENT?**
>
> **STOICISM**
>
> *A philosophy built around mastering your response to life rather than controlling life itself.*
>
> **Explore →**

Cards should use strong visual composition and subtle backgrounds rather than generic text boxes.

The visual system should be consistent across cards while allowing individual concepts to have distinct visual identities.

---

# 11. No Categories in the Feed

The user should not see:

> Philosophy
> Psychology
> Science
> History

as feed filters.

The feed should intentionally mix discoveries.

For example:

```text
Stoicism
↓
Ship of Theseus
↓
Cognitive Dissonance
↓
Nietzsche
↓
Fermi Paradox
↓
Absurdism
↓
Trolley Problem
```

This creates intellectual serendipity.

The underlying database may still store categories/types for organization and recommendation logic, but these categories should not dominate the user experience.

---

# 12. Content Discovery

The initial MVP will contain a finite curated content library.

Target:

**Approximately 500–1,000 discoveries.**

The system should not pretend to generate infinite content.

Instead, the feed should feel effectively continuous through:

* Intelligent ordering
* Related discoveries
* Contrasting discoveries
* Cross-topic exploration
* Previously unseen content
* Expansion into related concepts

Future versions may add additional content.

---

# 13. Content Creation

The runtime application should **not use generative AI to dynamically create explanations**.

AI may be used during the development/content-authoring process to:

* Draft initial content
* Structure content
* Generate candidate descriptions
* Identify relationships
* Suggest related ideas

However, content must be curated and stored before being delivered to users.

Runtime architecture:

```text
Curated Content
      ↓
Database
      ↓
API
      ↓
Android App
```

Not:

```text
User
 ↓
LLM
 ↓
Generated Philosophy
```

---

# 14. Content Data Structure

Each discovery should contain structured information.

Minimum fields:

```text
id
title
type
hook
short_description
detailed_description
background_style
key_people
core_ideas
real_world_example
related_discoveries
contrasting_discoveries
created_at
updated_at
```

The database should also support content status such as:

```text
draft
review
published
archived
```

Sources may be stored internally for content management and verification, but citations are **not displayed on feed cards**.

# 15. Detail Page

Tapping a discovery opens a detailed exploration page.

Example:

# STOICISM

### What is Stoicism?

A concise, accessible explanation.

### Where did it originate?

Historical context.

### Core Principles

Key ideas presented clearly.

### Key Philosophers

* Zeno
* Epictetus
* Seneca
* Marcus Aurelius

### Important Ideas

Links to related concepts.

### Everyday Example

A practical example that makes the philosophy understandable.

### Related Ideas

Concepts that connect naturally to Stoicism.

### Contrasting Perspective

A philosophy or concept that offers a substantially different perspective.

### Continue Exploring

A clear path to another discovery.

The detail page should remain readable and editorial rather than resembling an academic encyclopedia.

---

# 16. Related Ideas

Every discovery should be capable of linking to other discoveries.

Example:

```text
Stoicism
├── Marcus Aurelius
├── Epictetus
├── Dichotomy of Control
├── Amor Fati
├── Virtue Ethics
└── Epicureanism
```

Relationships should have semantic meaning where possible.

Examples:

* Related to
* Contrasts with
* Influenced by
* Influenced
* Associated with
* Example of
* Developed by
* Similar question
* Different answer

The relationship system should be extensible.

---

# 17. Contrasting Perspectives

This is a core product feature.

After exploring an idea, Phils should sometimes present an opposing or substantially different perspective.

Example:

### STOICISM

> Focus on controlling your response to events.

Then:

### Explore a contrasting perspective

**EPICUREANISM**

> A philosophy focused on tranquility, moderation, and freedom from unnecessary desires.

The purpose is not to determine which philosophy is "correct."

The purpose is to expose users to different ways of thinking.

Phils should avoid presenting philosophical disagreements as simple right/wrong answers.

---

# 18. Recommendation System

The MVP should use a deterministic recommendation system rather than a machine-learning model.

Inputs can include:

* Content already viewed
* Saved discoveries
* Discovery type
* Relatedness
* Contrasting relationships
* Recent exploration
* Previously unseen content
* Exploration diversity

The recommendation system should avoid repeatedly showing the same topic.

For example:

If the user explores several Stoicism concepts, the feed should not become:

```text
Stoicism
Stoicism
Stoicism
Stoicism
Stoicism
```

Instead:

```text
Stoicism
↓
Epictetus
↓
Existentialism
↓
Dichotomy of Control
↓
Absurdism
```

The algorithm should balance:

**Familiarity + novelty + diversity + relationship**

---

# 19. "Why Am I Seeing This?"

Each recommendation should optionally explain why it appeared.

Example:

> **Why this idea?**
>
> You recently explored Stoicism.
>
> This discovery explores a related question from a different philosophical perspective.

Possible reasons:

* Related to something recently viewed
* Contrasting perspective
* Connected to a saved idea
* Similar concept
* Introduces a new area
* Recommended for exploration diversity

The explanation should be concise and understandable.

---

# 20. Save Feature

Users can save discoveries.

There are intentionally:

* No likes
* No dislikes
* No public popularity score

Saving means:

> "I want to come back to this."

The user should have a saved-items section.

Example:

# Saved

* Stoicism
* Ship of Theseus
* Fermi Paradox
* Existentialism
* Cognitive Dissonance

Saving and unsaving should work immediately.

# 24. Offline Behavior

Because the backend is self-hosted and network availability can vary, the app should cache recently retrieved discoveries.

If the server becomes temporarily unavailable:

* Previously loaded discoveries remain accessible.
* Saved items remain accessible locally.
* The user should receive a clear indication that fresh content cannot currently be loaded.
* The app should retry when connectivity returns.

The MVP does not require complete offline synchronization of the entire database.

---

# 25. Backend

The backend will run entirely on the user's own server.

The exact implementation technology can be selected during technical design, but the architecture should provide:

### API responsibilities

* Feed retrieval
* Discovery retrieval
* Search
* Save/unsave
* User profile
* Exploration history
* Recommendation
* Related discovery retrieval
* Contrasting discovery retrieval

The API must be versioned.

Example:

```text
/api/v1/feed
/api/v1/discoveries/{id}
/api/v1/search
/api/v1/saved
/api/v1/profile
```

# 26. Database

PostgreSQL is the preferred primary database.

Core entities should include:

```text
users
discoveries
discovery_types
discovery_relationships
saved_discoveries
view_history
```

Potential future entities:

```text
content_sources
content_versions
recommendation_events
```

The database should use proper indexes and foreign keys.

---

# 27. Self-Hosted Deployment

All backend services must be deployable on the user's server.

Preferred deployment approach:

```text
Docker
├── API
├── PostgreSQL
└── Supporting services
```

The system must not require:

* Firebase
* Supabase
* AWS
* Vercel
* Cloud-hosted PostgreSQL
* Hosted authentication provider
* Third-party backend-as-a-service

External services may be used only when explicitly required and approved.

---

# 28. Security Requirements

The backend must:

* Use HTTPS in production
* Validate API input
* Never trust client-provided user IDs
* Protect authenticated endpoints
* Hash passwords if password authentication is implemented
* Avoid storing sensitive information unnecessarily
* Use environment variables for secrets
* Never hard-code credentials
* Apply database permissions appropriately
* Avoid exposing PostgreSQL directly to the public internet

---

# 29. Performance Requirements

The feed is the most performance-sensitive part of the application.

Target behavior:

* Fast initial feed load
* Smooth vertical swiping
* Preload upcoming cards
* Avoid unnecessary network calls
* Cache feed content
* Avoid blocking the UI thread
* Efficient image/background loading
* Pagination rather than downloading the entire content library

The user should not feel that each swipe is waiting for a server request.

# 31. Navigation

Initial navigation should remain minimal.

Suggested structure:

```text
                 PHILS
                   │
       ┌───────────┼───────────┐
       ▼           ▼           ▼
      Feed       Search       Profile
                               │
                               ▼
                             Saved
```

The feed should remain the primary destination.

Detail pages should feel like entering a discovery rather than navigating away from the product.

---

# 32. Authentication

Authentication should be kept simple for MVP.

A user account is necessary for:

* Saved discoveries
* History
* Personal profile
* Cross-device persistence in the future

The authentication system should be self-hosted.

No third-party authentication provider should be required.

If authentication complexity threatens the initial feed MVP, it should be implemented after the basic content experience is working.

---

# 33. Analytics

The MVP should track only product-useful events.

Potential events:

```text
discovery_viewed
discovery_opened
discovery_saved
discovery_unsaved
search_performed
related_discovery_opened
contrasting_discovery_opened
```

The system should avoid unnecessary personal tracking.

The primary purpose of analytics is to understand:

* Which ideas attract attention
* Which ideas are opened
* Which ideas are saved
* How users navigate between ideas

---

# 34. Content Quality

The product's credibility depends heavily on content quality.

Content should be:

* Clear
* Concise
* Accurate
* Neutral
* Accessible to beginners
* Free from unnecessary academic jargon

Philosophical positions should not be distorted into motivational slogans.

For example, the application should avoid reducing an entire philosophy to:

> "Stoicism = Don't care about anything."

Instead, explanations should preserve the actual meaning while remaining accessible.

---

# 35. MVP Success Criteria

The MVP is successful if a new user can:

1. Open Phils.
2. Immediately see an interesting discovery.
3. Swipe vertically to another discovery.
4. Continue discovering different ideas without selecting a category.
5. Tap an interesting discovery.
6. Read a detailed explanation.
7. Explore related ideas.
8. See a contrasting perspective.
9. Save an idea.
10. Search for a concept.
11. Return to previously saved ideas.
12. Continue using the app without the experience feeling like a conventional educational application.

# 36. Example End-to-End Journey

### Step 1

User opens Phils.

### Step 2

First card:

> **WHAT IF YOU COULD CONTROL YOUR REACTION, BUT NOT THE EVENT?**
>
> **STOICISM**
>
> *A philosophy built around mastering your response to life rather than controlling life itself.*

User swipes.

### Step 4

Next card:

> **IF EVERYTHING ABOUT A SHIP IS REPLACED, IS IT STILL THE SAME SHIP?**
>
> **SHIP OF THESEUS**
>

### Step 5

User taps the card.

### Step 6

Detailed page explains the thought experiment.

### Step 7

User discovers related concepts.

### Step 8

Phils presents:

> **Want another perspective?**

### Step 9

User explores a contrasting concept.

### Step 10

User saves it.

### Step 11

Later, the user opens Profile → Saved and finds the idea.

This entire journey should feel seamless.

# 38. Product Principles

The following principles should guide all future development.

### 1. Discovery over education

Phils should make learning feel accidental.

### 2. Curiosity over information density

The feed should create questions rather than answer everything immediately.

### 3. Serendipity over categorization

Users should encounter ideas outside their existing interests.

### 4. Depth on demand

The feed stays concise. Detail pages provide depth.

### 5. Different perspectives over definitive answers

Phils exposes competing ways of thinking rather than deciding what users should believe.

### 6. No engagement manipulation

The product should not copy the most harmful mechanics of social media.

### 7. Self-hosted by design

The user's server is the source of truth for the application's backend and data.

### 8. Build the MVP before the intelligence

A reliable content/feed/detail experience is more important than a sophisticated recommendation model.

# 40. Final Product Definition

> **Phils is an Android app that turns the familiar habit of infinite vertical scrolling into a way to discover ideas worth thinking about.**

The application should not tell users what to believe.

It should continually expose them to ideas they may never have encountered, provide enough context to spark curiosity, and give them an easy path to go deeper.

The fundamental loop is:

```text
OPEN
  ↓
DISCOVER
  ↓
SWIPE
  ↓
DISCOVER SOMETHING ELSE
  ↓
GET CURIOUS
  ↓
EXPLORE
  ↓
CONNECT IDEAS
  ↓
SEE ANOTHER PERSPECTIVE
  ↓
SAVE WHAT MATTERS
  ↓
CONTINUE DISCOVERING
```

**Phils is not an encyclopedia disguised as a feed.**

It is a **discovery engine disguised as scrolling.**
